# Taxes

Taxes are the government's share. A small business usually meets three kinds:
- **Taxes on sales** (sales tax or VAT): added to the price and collected for the
  government. This money is not your [[Revenue]], even though it passes through your till.
- **Payroll taxes:** paid on wages when [[Hiring]] people. They are part of staff [[Costs]].
- **Tax on profit** (income or corporate tax): paid on what is left after costs.

The names, rates and rules depend on the country and on how the business is set up. Check
local rules or ask an accountant before relying on any number.

**Corner Coffee** makes about $5,780 a month before tax on profit:
revenue $29,700 − variable costs $7,920 (6,600 drinks × $1.20) − fixed costs $16,000.
That is about $69,400 a year. At an *illustrative* 20% rate, that is about $13,900 a year
in tax on profit, leaving about $55,500.

Tax on profit is often paid months after the money was earned. That makes it a
[[Cash Flow]] trap: a café that spends all its profit can't pay the bill when it arrives.
A simple habit is to move about $1,160 a month (20% of profit) into a separate account.

If prices must include sales tax, it also affects [[Pricing]]: a $4.50 price that includes
tax leaves less than $4.50 for the café.
