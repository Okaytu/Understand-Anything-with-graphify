# Hiring

Hiring adds people to do the work in [[Operations]]. Wages are usually a fixed cost
(see [[Costs]]): you pay them whether sales are good or bad. So every hire has to earn
back what it costs.

**Corner Coffee** pays $9,500 a month in staff today: two baristas at peak hours and one
off-peak. Queues at 8 a.m. are getting longer, so the owner considers a part-time barista
for the morning rush at about $2,400 a month.

- Fixed costs rise from $16,000 to $18,400 a month.
- The [[Break-even Point]] rises from 162 to about 186 drinks a day ($18,400 ÷ $3.30 ÷ 30).
- To pay for itself, the hire must bring in $2,400 ÷ $3.30 ≈ 727 extra drinks a month,
  about 24 a day. That's people who would have left the queue, or regulars who now come
  back because it's faster.

That is the real question behind most hires: will this person protect or add enough sales
at our [[Gross Margin]] to cover their cost? Here it is plausible, because speed is part of
the [[Value Proposition]].

Two more things to check before hiring:
- **Timing:** wages leave every two weeks, before the extra sales arrive (see [[Cash Flow]]).
- **On-costs:** employer payroll [[Taxes]], insurance and training usually make a person
  cost more than their wage.
