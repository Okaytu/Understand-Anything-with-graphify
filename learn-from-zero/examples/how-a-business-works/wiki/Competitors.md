# Competitors

Competitors are the other choices your [[Customers]] have for the same need. That includes
direct rivals, substitutes (making coffee at home), and doing nothing. They limit what you
can charge ([[Pricing]]) and decide which parts of your [[Value Proposition]] actually matter.

**Corner Coffee** has a chain café across the street charging $5.00 a latte, and a new
independent café is about to open two blocks away.

Suppose the new café takes 30 drinks a day. Corner Coffee drops from 220 to 190 drinks,
still above its [[Break-even Point]] of 162, but daily profit falls from about $190 to
about $92 (28 × $3.30).

The tempting response is a price war: cut the latte to $4.00. That shrinks the
[[Gross Margin]] to $2.80 and moves break-even to $16,000 ÷ $2.80 ≈ 5,714 drinks a month,
about 190 a day. At 190 drinks the café would earn nothing.

The better response is to compete on what rivals can't easily copy: speed under three
minutes and a calm place to work. Then tell people about it through [[Marketing]].

Questions to ask about any competitor:
- Which of my customers would they win, and why?
- Can they sustain lower prices longer than I can?
- What do I do that they cannot easily copy?
